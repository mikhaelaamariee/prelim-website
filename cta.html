🔊 CALL TO ACTION

The window to set equity standards is closing.

University administrators, faculty senates, and the Commission on Higher Education must act now, before an inequitable detection regime becomes structurally entrenched, and before a generation of Filipino students is algorithmically defined as dishonest for writing legitimately in their second language.

Review the Framework →
THE ASK

This is not a rejection of AI tools. It is a demand for fairness

The adoption of AI detection tools in Philippine higher education is accelerating, and every semester without transparent disclosure standards is a semester in which students with legitimate ESL writing patterns risk wrongful academic penalties. The Transparency and Equity-Audit Framework simply demands that these tools be held to the same standards of accuracy and fairness we demand of human assessors.

WHY SUPPORT THIS PROPOSAL NOW?
01

AI tool adoption is outpacing policy

Most Philippine HEIs began using AI detection tools without any ESL-specific calibration, accuracy policy, or faculty training.

02

Wrongful accusations are already occurring

Documented cases in the Philippines already show AI detection results being used as grounds for investigation, alongside foundational research confirming false-positive rates severe enough to misclassify honest non-native English writing at scale.

03

SDG 10 is a national commitment

The Philippines is a signatory to the UN 2030 Agenda. Academic institutions share responsibility for reducing inequalities of outcome in education.

04

The solution is low-cost and evidence-based

Vendor disclosure requirements and policy standardization require no new funding, only institutional will and regulatory follow-through.

05

Delay compounds inequity

Every academic year that passes without standards is another year in which the most linguistically vulnerable students bear the highest risk.

✓

For university administrators & CHED policymakers

This framework is designed for practical institutional adoption. Implementation requires no external funding, no specialized infrastructure, and is fully compatible with existing CHED accreditation and quality-assurance frameworks. It is a governance reform, not a funding initiative.
