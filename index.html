THE THESIS

A biased, opaque adjudication system operating without oversight

"AI detection tools meant to protect academic integrity in Philippine universities instead penalize non-native English writers unfairly, proving that more surveillance isn't the same as more fairness."

As Philippine universities increasingly rely on AI-powered plagiarism and AI-writing detection tools, a critical gap in equity has emerged. Foundational research testing these systems on non-native English writing found error rates severe enough to misclassify honest student work as AI-generated, with average false-positive rates reaching 61.3% for essays written by non-native English speakers. While newer detectors have narrowed this gap, the risk of wrongful accusation remains real, especially for Filipino students who write academic English as a second language.

Compounding this technical flaw is an enforcement double standard. While students face investigation for suspected AI use, a 2025 global survey of higher education faculty found that 61% have used AI in their own teaching, often without the same disclosure or scrutiny expected of students (Digital Education Council, 2025). This inconsistency undermines the legitimacy of enforcement itself. This proposal introduces the Transparency and Equity-Audit Framework, a phased, evidence-based approach aligned with SDG 10 (Reduced Inequalities), demanding vendor disclosure of ESL error rates, unified AI-use policy, and ongoing institutional monitoring.
